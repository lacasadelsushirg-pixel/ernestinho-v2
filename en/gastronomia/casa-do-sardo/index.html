Casa do Sardo

Sardinian cuisine, breads, cured meats and a Mediterranean-style house in Santa Teresa.

Ernestinho GuideSanta Teresa

Identity and visual atmosphere

Casa do Sardo is not simply an Italian restaurant: its identity revolves around Sardinia, the home island of chef and owner Silvio Podda. It operates at 405 Rua São Cristóvão in a historic building. I would suggest it as a deliberate food-focused outing for travelers curious about a less familiar regional cuisine and willing to leave the usual Copacabana–Ipanema circuit.

History and concept

The official website says the restaurant was created to share Sardinian food culture and the meeting of land and sea flavors. That idea appears in the pastas, fish, seafood and meat dishes documented in guides and press reports. Mentioned specialties include seafood fregola, malloreddus with lamb ragù, culurgiones, clams, bottarga, octopus and lamb. I would use these examples to explain the cuisine, not promise a fixed menu.

My personal take as Ernestinho

If you have never tried Sardinian cuisine, ask staff how its pastas and recipes differ from more familiar Italian food. It can be a great unhurried meal for couples; with friends or family, sharing a starter and ordering two different mains lets you sample land and sea without overordering. I would reserve for larger groups because the website offers bookings and reviews mention busy periods.

What to order and why

I would first ask about fregola, an available handmade pasta, and the fish or seafood of the day. The website has also featured friturina di mare and panini, but choices depend on service time and the current menu. For meat lovers, Sardinian lamb dishes have been documented. I would not promise bottarga, culurgiones or any specific preparation without checking: availability changes, and prices quoted in old articles are not current rates.

Experience by time of day

The atmosphere is described as welcoming and distinctive, inside a historic house in São Cristóvão. I would not treat it as a quick stop or an impersonal modern dining room. The official hours previously consulted listed Tuesday–Saturday, noon to 10 p.m., and Sunday, noon to 4 p.m., but I would confirm before setting out. If ordering wine, check the list and budget first: some reviews note drinks can add significantly to the bill.

My specific advice

My advice is to book, check the current menu and ask what is actually available that day. Mention allergies or restrictions before ordering, especially for seafood, dairy or pasta dishes. Do not confuse Casa do Sardo with Osteria dei Sardi, Philia Sarda or product shops: these are different businesses. A clear listing matters because quick searches can mix up their names and direct visitors elsewhere.

Casa do Sardo offers a Sardinian-inspired dining experience in São Cristóvão. Ask which pastas and seafood or meat dishes are available rather than assuming a fixed menu.
