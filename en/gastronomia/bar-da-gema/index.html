Bar da Gema

Boteco cuisine and Tijuca memories, with snacks to share.

Ernestinho GuideTijuca

Identity and visual atmosphere

This listing refers to Bar da Gema, founded by Luiza Souza and Leandro Amaral and now located at 134 Rua Dona Zulmira around Maracanã/Tijuca. It should not be confused with Carioca da Gema, the samba venue in Lapa. The focus here is boteco cooking: an informal atmosphere, hearty dishes and traditional recipes reinterpreted with humor and creativity. I would describe it as a place to share food and discover a more elaborate menu than at an ordinary bar.

History and concept

Bar da Gema opened in 2009 on Rua Barão de Mesquita and moved to its current address in 2023. The new phase kept the dishes that had built its reputation, but in a different space. That move matters to travelers because older addresses still appear online. Rather than focus on past awards, I would tell the story of a boteco that draws on Brazilian culinary memory and turns it into dishes with their own names and character.

My personal take as Ernestinho

I would choose Bar da Gema for a casual meal with friends, especially a group that enjoys bold flavors and ordering several dishes to share. It would not be my pick for a quiet dinner or a light menu. The food has a boteco spirit and many signature dishes are substantial. My ideal visit would start with a moderate appetite, listening to staff recommendations and ordering gradually rather than filling the table immediately.

What to order and why

Among the documented specialties is Fondue da Gema, a creamy cornmeal-and-cheese dish served with pork cracklings, sausage and ribs; the name is playful, not a reference to traditional Alpine fondue. Published dishes also include Coração Amargurado, with chicken hearts and jiló, polenta with oxtail, and the house coxinha. I would ask what remains available and on which days, since menus, ingredients and promotions change and old articles are not guarantees.

Experience by time of day

The venue is better suited to an unhurried outing than a fifteen-minute stop. Articles and reviews mention crowds at popular times, but they cannot guarantee waiting times or service speed. With a group, I would check whether reservations are accepted and ask about portion sizes. I would not quote prices from old reports: sharing two snacks is very different from ordering mains and several drinks.

My specific advice

If anyone avoids meat or has allergies, I would contact the restaurant before visiting. Many of its best-known recipes contain pork, beef or chicken, and a boteco kitchen may also involve sauces, shared frying oil and cross-contact. For drinks, I would ask the team for a pairing with richer dishes such as angu, pork cracklings or oxtail, rather than inventing a signature cocktail that is not on the current menu.

Before heading to Bar da Gema, check that your map shows 134 Rua Dona Zulmira in the Maracanã area. Older recommendations may still mention its previous address on Rua Barão de Mesquita. That small detail can save you a wasted journey. To order well, ask which snacks are being prepared and what today's specialties are; the menu and prices can change.

What to combine nearby

The current address is 134 Rua Dona Zulmira, not the former one on Barão de Mesquita. You could combine the visit with a Maracanã-related outing if the schedules really work, but I would not claim the bar is right beside the stadium. Metro, train, taxi or a ride-hailing app may work depending on your starting point; use the current address in your map and plan the return, especially after dark.

This recommendation makes sense as part of a Tijuca and Maracanã itinerary rather than an improvised stop on Copacabana's waterfront. If you are coming from the South Zone, allow for travel time and confirm opening hours. That way you can enjoy a neighborhood boteco without relying on outdated information.
