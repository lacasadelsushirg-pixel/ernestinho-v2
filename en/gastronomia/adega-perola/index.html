Ernestinho Carioca · Copacabana

Adega Pérola

Historic Copacabana boteco with a display of preserves and seafood snacks.

BotecoSeafoodSardinesSnacksBudget-friendly

Visual identity and atmosphere

Adega Pérola operates at Rua Siqueira Campos, 138-A, Copacabana, and the meal begins by looking at the display. Riotur dates its origin to 1957, founded by Portuguese brothers; after nearly closing, regular customers took over and preserved the house. It is now recognized as Carioca Cultural Heritage. I would recommend it to travelers who enjoy choosing small bites, sharing, and comparing flavors rather than having a single main course in a conventional restaurant meal.

History and concept

Documented choices include octopus vinaigrette, fried shrimp, mussels, sardines, roe, fish escabeches, garlic in vinaigrette, buffalo mozzarella, asparagus, and rollmops—marinated sardines rolled around onion. Visit Rio also describes “Pérolas do Mar,” a mix of scallops, shrimp, octopus, and mussels. VEJA reported dozens of choices in 2026. Supply can vary, so I would first inspect the display and ask what is freshest.

My personal take as Ernestinho

For a first round I would choose three or four different things: one shellfish item, one fish or preserve, and something vegetable- or dairy-based for balance. Octopus vinaigrette is a documented classic; rollmop offers a stronger, saltier flavour. I would not order every famous item at once. For couples or friends, small portions and repeating only what you like make more sense than crowding the table before understanding size and price.

What to order and why

It is especially important here to understand how pricing works. Some products may be sold by unit, portion, or weight; ask the rate before accepting a large serving. Spending changes significantly with seafood, preserves, and number of rounds. Press prices from 2026 are dated references. Beer often suits the boteco format, but I would check the current selection of beer, wine and other drinks rather than invent unverified labels.

Experience by time of day

For a couple, the display creates conversation and discovery; with friends, the format allows more sampling; with children or people who avoid fish, I would check whether enough simple alternatives exist. With shellfish, fish, or mollusk allergies, do not assume separate handling: ask clearly about cross-contact. Diners who dislike vinegar, preserved foods, or strong marine flavors may find the experience less suitable.

My specific advice

The Siqueira Campos location fits naturally into a Copacabana day. Metro, bus, taxi, or ride-hailing can get you close, after which you can continue toward the beach or Bairro Peixoto. The bar and dining room may fill at busy times; historic fame does not guarantee immediate seating. I would stay flexible and avoid a critical reservation immediately afterward. Hours and weekly closing should be confirmed through the current channel or phone.

At Adega Pérola, you can share small dishes selected from the display counter. Ask what has been prepared that day and how each item is priced, as prices and availability may change.

What to combine nearby

Adega Pérola's strength is a very specific identity: nearly seven decades of history and a display that makes choosing part of the experience. The limitation is less predictable spending than a fixed-price menu. My advice is to look first, ask the per-unit or per-weight price, order a small first round, and then repeat. That preserves the spirit of a historic boteco without turning curiosity into an unexpected bill.

Because it is in Copacabana, you can easily pair the visit with a walk through the neighborhood or along the waterfront. In a small group, ordering modest portions first and sharing helps everyone discover more flavors without wasting food.
